## The baked meshes (`node scripts/bake/check-mesh.mjs`)

Both tiers are watertight: 0 open edges on every part (an earlier bake dropped sliver triangles and left a few hundred cracks in the head, which showed as white specks along the face-plate edge; triangles are now wound from the grid topology and only index-degenerate ones are removed). The final head has 111k triangles (87k before its edge was refined) and 4 non-manifold edges; the lite head 51k and none. The non-manifold edges are a known trait of surface-nets meshes; none is visible.

The hood's edge used to be a step in the sculpt (the plate "tucked" 0.028 below the hood across no distance at all). The 0.021-unit grid cut it as a staircase, so once the head turned the colour boundary zig-zagged over it: teeth and white specks along the whole seam, which a front-on audit cannot show. The edge is now a continuous wall (0.04 units wide, 0.016 deep) and the bake splits every head triangle within 0.1 units of the plate's outline once more (`scripts/bake/refine.mjs`).

## The seam when the head turns (`node scripts/dev/turn-check.mjs`)

The plate's outline (where the hood's red takes over from the charcoal, found to a tenth of a pixel by walking in from the hood along 360 rays from the plate's centre) is compared with a smooth Fourier fit of itself; bright specks on the plate are counted. At 2× density and yaw ±0.5 (scored): mean deviation 0.07 px at both, 95th percentile 0.17 and 0.16 px, 0 and 7 specks in about 77 000 plate pixels. The same measurement on the old stepped mesh gave 0.37 and 0.44 px mean (95th percentile 1.40 and 1.82 px). At 1× density and at larger yaws the crevice is narrower than a pixel or darker than the shadowed wall, so those views are printed but not scored (1×: 0.17 px at p95; yaw 0.9 at 2×: 0.81 px). Looked at by eye at 3 to 10× (nearest neighbour) at 1× and 2× and at yaw 0, ±0.3, ±0.5, 0.9, ±1.2, ±1.5, head pitched ±0.35 and rolled ±0.3, the outline is a clean thin curve everywhere. (The locator used to be the darkest point of the crevice line; once the lip that brightens along the chin was modelled, two dark bands a few pixels apart made that jump from ray to ray and read as roughness that the eye cannot see.) Checked on the development machine's GPU (Apple M5 Pro, Metal) only.

- **Chest cord.** The cord's wraps are about 2.7 px apart at 350 px/unit. Below ~300 px/unit (a 1× display, or the page's stage on a 2× one) they are finer than two pixels, and sampling them aliased into a checkerboard that shimmered as the figure moved; they now fade out with their own spatial frequency (screen-space gradient of the uv along the cord) into a plain cord of the same mean colour, which is why the contrast row reads 1.7 against 8.8 at 246 px/unit (the artwork, rescaled, keeps faint aliased stripes). At 350 px/unit the wraps match the artwork (std 14.4 against 13.9 along the cord).
- **Hood-to-plate seam.** The red hood meets the charcoal plate through a rolled lip (real geometry), a thin dark crevice about 0.01 units wide, and a plate that darkens as it tucks in under the hood. Mean luminance across that seam, binned by distance from the plate's edge in 0.004-unit steps on the top, left, right and bottom sides, differs from the artwork's by 5.3 levels (of 255) on average at 350 px/unit; away from the crevice itself (within 0.008 units of it) the top, left and right sides follow the artwork's ramp within about 10 levels, 13 at the far edge of the window on the right; the bottom lip is within 12 levels of the artwork's (93 against 105 at 0.006 units, 102 against 111 at 0.010) and the crevice itself is a little wide and deep on the hood side. The metric is `seam` in `eval.mjs`.
- **Plate texture.** Fetching the nap tile at screen density blurred its finest filaments into cloudy blotches; the tiles are now pre-emphasised (`scripts/nap/*-emphasis.json`, fitted by `scripts/dev/fit-nap-spectrum.py` on the displayed luminance of feature-free windows) so that the rendered plate and hood have the artwork's spectrum at 350, 246 and 123 px/unit (individual frequency bands between 0.7 and 1.0 of the artwork's energy on the plate, 0.73 to 1.23 on the hood at 246). Skew / kurtosis of the plate's fibre band: 0.41 / 3.3 live against 0.41 / 3.2 for the forehead, 0.33 / 3.2 against 0.42 / 3.2 between the eyes (350 px/unit). Plate colour in linear light is within 2 to 4% per channel of the artwork's on the forehead, between the eyes and on the chin.
- Front silhouette against the artwork's alpha at rest: IoU 0.970 (0.979 against the symmetrised fit target); profile against the turnaround sheet: 0.960 (head 0.970: the plate's dome reaches the profile's apex).
- Facial features against the artwork (IoU of the segmented shapes at 350 px/unit): eyes 0.989 and 0.977, cheeks 0.963 and 0.956, mouth 0.913; centroids within 0.005 units.
- Poster and live model line up on the stage: figure height ratio 0.995, centre offset 0-0.5 px, at 2×, 1× and 390 px wide, so the fade from artwork to live model does not jump.
- Known misses, in order of visibility: the ears are rounder than the artwork's and lack its inner seam (the artwork's right ear has a thin seam along its inner edge); the bottom edge of the feet is about 25 levels lighter than the artwork's; the hood's roll around the plate is a smooth step plus shading, not a fully modelled padded tube with an undercut, and its fibres do not interlock with the plate's the way the painted ones do; the artwork's pale filaments on the hood are brighter and sparser than the model's; fibre contrast on the chest, leg and arm patches is 15-40% above the artwork's while the belly and ears match.
